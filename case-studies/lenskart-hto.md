# Lenskart@Home — Growth & Adoption Case Study

**Role:** Product Manager  
**Domain:** O2O Commerce · Eyewear · Activation · Conversion · Retention

## Context

Lenskart@Home (HTO) brings an eyewear trial experience to a customer’s home. My product scope covered growth, conversion, customer experience, and retention across the journey—from booking through post-purchase support.

## The product problem

In a typical week, **80% of Home Trial landing-page visitors dropped off at step one**, before selecting an address or slot. Customer calls with people who had shown intent but abandoned at that point revealed that the core barrier was not the service itself: customers lacked confidence in a kit they could not influence.

## Hypothesis and experiment

**Hypothesis:** Giving customers control over the frames in their trial would improve completed Home Trial bookings, even before adding inventory complexity.

**Pilot:** A **three-week, pan-India pilot** reused existing product-listing surfaces so customers could select frames from the agent’s existing kit. The primary metric was completed Home Trial booking conversion among landing-page visitors, compared with the weekly baseline.

**Result:** Completed Home Trial bookings increased **7%** during the pilot. This validated that control—not simply more frame variety—was the lever worth scaling.

## Product approach

I worked across:
- Customer and funnel-behaviour analysis
- Problem discovery and prioritisation
- Experiment design and measurement
- Product requirements and solution design
- Engineering and cross-functional execution
- Measurement of customer and business outcomes

## Scale and outcomes

The production experience enabled customers to browse inventory from their nearby Lenskart store and select frames for their home trial. It launched across **five cities** and contributed **₹1.8Cr in incremental annualised revenue** through higher Home Trial bookings.

This work is one component of **₹6Cr+ in incremental annualised revenue** from multiple Lenskart@Home initiatives.

## O2O ownership and retention

I also worked on territory-based store ownership for online, offline, and home-trial customers. Stores completed 48-hour post-delivery check-ins to resolve issues early and create a clear service relationship for online customers.

Over one month, NPS for orders where the assigned store completed a check-in rose from **41 to 60**. The no-call control cohort remained low. Return-rate impact is still being measured.

## What I learned

The strongest adoption opportunities were often not new features. They came from understanding where customer intent broke, testing the smallest intervention that could restore confidence, and building the operating model needed to make the behaviour repeatable.
